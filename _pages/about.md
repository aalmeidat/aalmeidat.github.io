I study how dwarf galaxies are assembled, using the motions and chemistry of individual stars in the Magellanic Clouds. My central question is what builds the outskirts of a dwarf galaxy: its own star formation, mergers with smaller systems, or the tides of the larger galaxy it orbits. The Small Magellanic Cloud, which the Large Magellanic Cloud is pulling apart, is the closest place to test this. With APOGEE spectra and *Gaia* astrometry, I showed that the nearer of two groups of stars in the SMC's eastern periphery shares the chemistry of the SMC's center, supporting the idea that the LMC pulled these stars out of the inner SMC ([Almeida et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stae373)). I now separate the overlapping stellar populations of the SMC with mixture models that use every star's measurement uncertainties, and test whether each component the data suggest is actually required. I am also measuring how often red giants in the Clouds have close binary companions, and whether that fraction changes with metallicity ([interactive orbits](/magellanic-joker-orbits/)).

Before graduate school I spent six years at Las Campanas Observatory with the APOGEE-2S survey, as its Lead Observer from fall 2016: 232 survey nights on the du Pont telescope, the observer training and certification program, and the operator safety plan. In SDSS-V I was Deputy Commissioning Scientist for the southern site (2020–2021) and then worked on survey operations and data quality (2021–2025); more on the [survey operations](/operations/) page. I am a PhD candidate in Astronomy at the University of Virginia, advised by Steven Majewski, and expect to defend in March 2027. I hold an M.Sc. in Physics from Universidad Andrés Bello and a B.Sc. in Physics from Universidad de Chile.
